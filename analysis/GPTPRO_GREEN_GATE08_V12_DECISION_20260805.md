# 6. Scientific Interpretation of the Rank-Four and Rank-Five Evidence

## 6.1 What the combined evidence establishes

The two runs used different donor noun populations and different frozen protocols, yet both found:

1. a large boundary singular direction;
2. a weak adjacent separation;

The boundary singular value is more than half the leading singular value in both cases. This is inconsistent with the intended picture of a sharply isolated four- or five-dimensional donor mechanism.

## 6.2 Donor-selection defect

A donor-selection defect is not the best explanation.

The v1.2 population was:

- entirely fresh;
- balanced across roles;
- prompt-disjoint;
- noun-disjoint from evaluation;
- noun-disjoint from the rank-four donor set;
- deterministic and hashed.

The same qualitative failure under a fresh population makes an accidental lexical choice substantially less plausible.

This does not prove that every conceivable donor distribution would have the same spectrum. It does show that repeatedly searching donor populations until a favorable gap appears would turn the scientific object into a selected artifact.

## 6.3 Smooth or clustered covariance spectrum

The admissible inference is:

> The donor covariance does not present a stable canonical boundary at rank four or rank five.

A stronger statement that the entire spectrum is smooth is not justified because the v1.2 full singular array was not serialized.

Classical eigenspace perturbation results make the relevant issue explicit: stability of a selected singular-vector block is controlled by separation from adjacent singular values. When the denominator is small, a fixed sample perturbation can produce a large subspace rotation. 

The rank-four and rank-five evidence therefore undermines the **uniqueness and stability** of the selected PCA projector, even without proving a globally smooth spectrum.

## 6.4 Failure of fixed-rank PCA as the scientific object

This is the central interpretation.

A donor PCA projector is a property of:

- a selected donor distribution;
- a selected chord definition;
- a selected covariance weighting;
- a selected truncation rank;
- an eigengap at the selected boundary.

It is not automatically a property of the transformer’s causal mechanism.

The two stops show that the intended causal object cannot be grounded by declaring rank four or rank five and then requiring the donor data to isolate that boundary.

## 6.5 Failure of the broader matched-bypass theory

No.

The matched-bypass identity is local and directional. It requires a probe-complete finite design for the upstream gate differential; it does not require that the probes be PCA singular vectors.

The donor PCA supplied one possible finite coordinate system. Its failure removes that coordinate construction, not the causal factorization.

## 6.6 Broader mechanistic interpretation

A plausible, but not directly proven, explanation is that block-10 residual variation induced by different year/noun corruptions is distributed across many comparable directions. That is compatible with recent work emphasizing that learned representations and causal mechanisms need not be concentrated in a uniquely recoverable low-dimensional Euclidean subspace.

ICLR 2024 work demonstrated that successful subspace interventions can activate unintended parallel pathways and therefore do not, by themselves, identify a unique causal direction.  Recent preprints further emphasize hidden interaction terms in multi-mediator patching and non-identifiability of representation-level properties from behavior alone. 

Those papers do not prove the present diagnosis. They reinforce the need to replace a descriptive covariance subspace with an explicitly identified causal operator.

## 9.3 Basis-Free Matched-Bypass Identification Theorem

**Theorem 1 — Basis-Free Matched-Bypass Identification.**

Assume \(F_s\), \(a_{sj}\), and \(\psi\) are twice continuously differentiable near their anchors, and that the path and matched-control interventions are constructed exactly as above. Then, for every \(u\in V\),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta H_{sj}(u)
=
C_{sj}
\langle g_{sj},u\rangle.
}
\]

If:

\[
C_{sj}\neq0,
\]

then the gate differential is identified as a linear functional:

\[
\boxed{
\langle g_{sj},u\rangle
=
\frac{
\left\langle
C_{sj},
\Delta H_{sj}(u)
\right\rangle
}{
\|C_{sj}\|_2^2
}
\qquad
\text{for every queried }u.
}
\]

The selected gate path operator is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_{sj}:V\rightarrow O,
\qquad
\mathscr P_{sj}(u)
=
G_{sj}
\langle g_{sj},u\rangle.
}
\]

Equivalently, under Euclidean coordinates:

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_{sj}
=
G_{sj}g_{sj}^{\top},
}
\]

a rank-at-most-one \(k\times d\) ambient operator.

### Proof

Set:

\[
b=c_j\psi'(a_0),
\]

\[
L=DF_s(0),
\]

and let:

\[
B=D^2F_s(0)
\]

be the symmetric vector-valued Hessian bilinear form.

For the path system, the downstream residual increment is:

\[

=
v+
c_j
\left[
\psi(a_{sj}(v)+z)-\psi(a_0)
\right].

At the origin, in direction \(u\),

\[
D_vw^P(0,0)[u]
=
u+b\langle g_{sj},u\rangle,
\]

\[
\partial_zw^P(0,0)=b,
\]

and

\[
D_v\partial_zw^P(0,0)[u]
=
c_j\psi''(a_0)
\langle g_{sj},u\rangle.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\begin{aligned}

\right]\\

\[
D_vw^C(0,0)[u]=u,
\]

\[
\partial_zw^C(0,0)=b,

\[
D_v\partial_zw^C(0,0)[u]=0.

\[

=
B[u,b].

\[
\begin{aligned}

&=
\langle g_{sj},u\rangle
\left(

+
L[c_j\psi''(a_0)]

\end{aligned}
\]

The term in parentheses is exactly:

\[
\partial_{zz}^2Y^P_{sj}(0,0)
=
C_{sj}.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\Delta H_{sj}(u)
=
C_{sj}\langle g_{sj},u\rangle.
\]

If \(C_{sj}\neq0\), projection onto the observed vector \(C_{sj}\) yields the unique scalar coefficient. Finally:

\[
G_{sj}
=
L[b],
\]

so the computational edge product for upstream direction \(u\) is:

\[
G_{sj}\langle g_{sj},u\rangle.
\]

This proves the result.

\[
